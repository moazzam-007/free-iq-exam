To build a scientifically credible online intelligence assessment, you must abandon the static "quiz" structure used by commercial websites and adopt modern psychometric engineering. The gold standard for cognitive assessment is the Cattell-Horn-Carroll (CHC) theory of cognitive abilities, which posits a hierarchical structure culminating in a general intelligence factor ($g$). For a streamlined online test, you must isolate the most strongly $g$-loaded domain: **Fluid Intelligence (Gf)**.

Fluid Intelligence is the ability to solve novel reasoning problems independent of prior knowledge, whereas Crystallized Intelligence (Gc) relies on acquired knowledge and vocabulary. To eliminate severe cultural and language biases, a global online test must strictly avoid Gc (Irwing & Hughes, 2018).

## 1. Validated Item Types & Generation

The most scientifically robust item type for measuring Gf is the **Matrix Reasoning** task, popularized by Raven's Progressive Matrices. Research demonstrates that matrix reasoning accounts for the vast majority of variance in non-verbal fluid intelligence testing (Bilker et al., 2012).

### Algorithmic Item Generation (AIG)

To prevent cheating and answer-key sharing, you cannot rely on a static bank of questions. You must use Algorithmic Item Generation (AIG) to dynamically generate matrices on the fly. As established in classical cognitive research (e.g., Carpenter, Just, & Shell, 1990), matrix items should be generated procedurally using combinations of five logical rules:

1. **Constant in a row/column:** A shape or feature remains identical across an axis.
2. **Quantitative pairwise progression:** Size, position, or number of elements increases or decreases by a constant mathematical interval.
3. **Figure addition/subtraction:** Shape A + Shape B = Shape C (overlapping lines either combine or cancel out).
4. **Distribution of three values:** Three distinct shapes or colors are distributed such that each row and column contains exactly one of each.
5. **Distribution of two values:** A logical XOR (exclusive OR) operation where visual elements overlap.

By systematically varying the rule complexity (combining 1 to 4 rules per item) and adding perceptual distractors, you computationally control the inherent difficulty of every generated item without requiring human authors.

## 2. The Open-Source Revolution: The ICAR Database

Proprietary tests like the WAIS or Raven's are strictly copyrighted and aggressively litigated. However, the academic community has solved this barrier to entry. The **International Cognitive Ability Resource (ICAR)** is a public-domain, scientifically validated open-source cognitive test battery.

## 3. Psychometric Engineering: IRT and CAT

Standard online tests use Classical Test Theory (simply summing up correct answers), which is highly inaccurate. A credible test must use **Item Response Theory (IRT)** and **Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT)** (Chen et al., 2021).

In IRT, the probability $P$ of a person answering correctly is a function of their latent ability ($\theta$) and the item's parameters. For a robust online test featuring multiple-choice matrices, use the **3-Parameter Logistic (3PL) Model**:

$$P(\theta) = c + \frac{1 - c}{1 + e^{-a(\theta - b)}}$$

* **$b$ (Difficulty):** Where the item functions best on the ability scale.
* **$a$ (Discrimination):** How sharply the item distinguishes between high and low ability candidates at the threshold.
* **$c$ (Guessing):** The probability of getting it right by random chance (e.g., 0.125 for an 8-option matrix).

**CAT Implementation:** Instead of giving everyone 40 static items, CAT dynamically selects the next item based on the user's running $\theta$ estimate (Li, Gibbons, & Rockova, 2025). If a user gets an item right, the algorithm serves a harder item (higher $b$). If they fail, it serves an easier item. This cuts test length by up to 50% while maintaining tighter standard error margins.

> **Key Insight:** To maintain a stable guessing parameter ($c$), procedural distractor options must be generated by applying incomplete rule logic (e.g., applying the matrix row rule but mathematically ignoring the column rule). Randomly generated distractors are too easily spotted by intelligent test-takers.

## 4. Security & Unproctored Internet Testing (UIT)

Online cognitive assessments suffer from score inflation due to unproctored internet testing (UIT) environments.

1. **Cognitive Fatigue & Order:** Administer the most cognitively demanding fluid reasoning sections first to mitigate careless responding caused by test fatigue, which heavily skews your algorithmic difficulty parameters (Akhtar & Kovacs, 2023).
2. **Item Exposure Control:** Use binary search algorithms and non-parametric termination rules to ensure no two users receive the exact same sequence of questions, neutralizing screenshot sharing (Li & Zheng, 2024).
3. **Speededness vs. Power:** Implement time limits per item based on log-normal response time modeling. A strict time limit (e.g., 60-90 seconds for a complex matrix) prevents users from crowdsourcing answers or building physical models.

## 5. Norming, Scoring, and Validation

Raw scores must be converted to the standardized IQ scale (Mean = 100, Standard Deviation = 15).

* **The Sample:** A credible norming sample requires $N \ge 1,000$ strictly stratified across age, education, and geography. Convenience sampling (e.g., posting to Reddit) will severely skew your norms upward by 10-15 points, invalidating the test.
* **Continuous Norming:** Because fluid intelligence naturally declines after age 25, use continuous norming (e.g., Taylor polynomials) to model the age trajectory, avoiding harsh, artificial percentile jumps at arbitrary age cutoffs.
* **Construct Validity:** Standard intelligence tests should demonstrate positive correlations with related real-world constructs like academic performance, occupational attainment, or creative achievement, though meta-analyses are required to establish true statistical effect sizes in these domains (Karwowski et al., 2021).

## 6. Audit of the Current Landscape

Most commercial tests fail the scientific criteria listed above:

* **Mensa Practice Tests:** High construct validity, but they rely heavily on Classical Test Theory instead of IRT. They are mostly static "power tests" without dynamic routing.
* **OpenPsychometrics (ICAR16):** Highly scientifically valid items, but static. Completely susceptible to answer-key sharing.
* **Commercial Scam Sites (e.g., IQTest.com):** Use pseudo-random questions, no IRT calibration, rely on the Barnum effect, and artificially inflate scores ($130+$) specifically to incentivize users to pay for a PDF certificate.
