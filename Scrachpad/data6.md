## 4. Reddit / community demand and complaints

Direct Reddit thread access was unreliable in this search session (the engine surfaced SEO/aggregator content instead of live threads for most Reddit-targeted queries), so I'm **not** going to fabricate specific thread quotes. What I *can* verify reliably, and what's arguably more decision-useful than anecdotal Reddit comments, is the **Trustpilot complaint corpus**, which is dated, sourced, and shows an extremely consistent pattern across *multiple, unrelated* competitor domains:

- **myiq.co.uk**: TrustScore 1.1/5 across 226 reviews; repeated pattern of £0.50–£1 "just pay to see your result," followed by an undisclosed £24.99–£29.99/month recurring charge [Verified — dk.trustpilot.com/review/myiq.co.uk, nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/myiq.co.uk, dated Feb–Mar 2026].
- **my-iq-tests.com**: same pattern, $29.99/month recurring after a $1 "unlock result" charge [Verified — ca.trustpilot.com/review/my-iq-tests.com].
- **quickiqtest.org**: complaints describe a "free" test that demands email then a $19.99–£14.99 paywall to reveal the score [Verified — nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/quickiqtest.org, fr.trustpilot.com/review/quickiqtest.org].
- **braintester.net**: identical €0.50 → €39.90/month pattern [Verified — trustpilot.com/review/braintester.net].
- **test-iq-online.com / "EFAQ.com"**: reviewers describe outright bank-detail harvesting complaints [Verified — ca.trustpilot.com/review/test-iq-online.com].

**This is the single strongest, best-evidenced finding in this whole research pass.** The "free IQ test" niche in the US/UK/EU market has a *structural trust problem* — a meaningful fraction of the ranking and advertised competitor set uses a dark-pattern subscription trap. This is not a one-off; it's the dominant complaint theme across five independent, unrelated domains, all with hundreds of reviews.

**Direct implication for your site:** "genuinely free, no payment method ever requested, no email required to see your score" isn't just a nice-to-have positioning line — it's a real, currently-underserved trust gap with search demand already forming around it (Cluster B and I above), and it's the exact kind of claim that earns organic backlinks from "best free IQ test, no scam" roundup articles, Reddit recommendation threads, and personal-finance/scam-warning content that already exists to warn people off the subscription-trap sites.

## 6. Content/tool gaps a minimal static site can actually exploit

1. **A genuinely free test with zero payment-method request, ever.** Given §4, this is your strongest single differentiator — cheap to build, structurally impossible for the dark-pattern competitors to credibly copy without abandoning their business model.
2. **A published methodology/transparency page** (reliability coefficient, described norm sample, confidence interval on the score) — confirmed by our earlier research that literally none of the audited competitors do this. Directly reuses the psychometric design work from your earlier request (ICAR-based items, IRT scoring, CHC domain structure).
3. **Sub-domain landing pages for each CHC-style test type** (matrix reasoning, verbal reasoning, working memory, processing speed) mirroring 123test's proven internal-linking pattern, each targeting its own long-tail cluster (Cluster G) and cross-linking to the main test — multiplies your indexable surface area without diluting focus.
4. **A dedicated, honestly-labeled "Mensa practice test" page** that's explicit about not being an admission-qualifying test (avoids the legal/trust risk some competitors blur) while still capturing Cluster E volume.
5. **A short-form "10-minute" variant** distinct from your full test, directly targeting Cluster C — this is a static, low-engineering-cost page (subset of your item bank) that captures a distinct intent (people who bounce off 30–40 question tests).
6. **A comparison/explainer page addressing "why is my IQ test asking me to pay?"** — directly SEO-targets the trust-gap queries in §5 while implicitly positioning your test against the scam pattern, and is genuinely link-worthy content for "scam warning" round-ups.
7. **An age/percentile chart page** — high-intent, snippet-friendly, cheap to build as a static reference table, and a natural internal-link magnet from your results page ("see where this score falls on the age-adjusted percentile chart").
