### The strongest findings

**The head term is enormous but difficult.** Current third-party datasets disagree on the exact number—which is normal because their methodologies differ—but they put `iq test` roughly in the **309K–550K monthly range**, with `free iq test` around **74K–76K**. Semrush currently shows 123test ranking around #3 for `iq test` and #2 for `free iq test`; Ahrefs shows roughly 48K and 22K monthly traffic respectively going to 123test from those terms. ([AhrefsTop][1])

**There is evidence that a smaller, focused domain can still compete.** Ahrefs' current snapshot for iqtest.com shows roughly **DR42 / 817 referring sites**, yet it appears around the top five for `iq test`. That does not mean a brand-new domain can immediately rank for the head term, but it does argue against treating domain strength as the only ranking variable. ([AhrefsTop][2])

**The best initial wedge is not simply “free IQ test.”** It is:

> **Free + no email + no signup + instant result + transparent accuracy/limitations**

That positioning directly attacks the major consumer complaints I found: questionable scores, score inflation, inconsistent tests, paid result reveals/subscriptions, and uncertainty about what an online score actually means. Current Reddit discussions repeatedly surface those issues, including explicit complaints about $1-entry/subscription funnels and people comparing very different scores across online tests. ([Reddit][3])

**Trust can become the SEO moat.** ARealMe is unusually explicit about its limitations: its online test is unsupervised, its norms describe its own participant population rather than a census-weighted population, and results should not be interpreted like a clinical assessment. That transparency is actually a competitive opportunity because many consumer sites make much stronger-sounding claims with much less methodological detail. ([A Real Me][4])

**OpenPsychometrics is the opposite competitive archetype:** substantially stronger research/academic signaling but a less mainstream consumer experience. Its current Semrush snapshot shows roughly **820K visits, 307K organic traffic and ~251K US traffic**, with a large backlink footprint. That creates a very interesting gap: combine consumer-grade UX with research-grade transparency. ([Semrush][5])

**CogniFit and Test-Guide illustrate additional gaps.** CogniFit currently ranks for `iq test free` and `free iq test` despite being fundamentally a broader brain-training ecosystem, while Test-Guide has a comparatively modest authority score but gets about **86.7% of its traffic from the US**. This supports a strategy centered on tight US search intent rather than trying to become a giant general “brain test” site. ([Semrush][6])

### The keyword strategy I recommend

The report builds an **82-query semantic universe** rather than pretending there is a finite “complete keyword list.” The major clusters are:

`iq test` → `free iq test` → `no email/no signup` → `quick/10 minute` → `accurate/real/legit` → `iq score` → `iq percentile` → `good IQ` → `Mensa` → `questions/practice` → `matrix/reasoning` → `spatial/numerical/verbal` → `kids/adults/age` → `online vs clinical/WAIS`.

Only a small subset currently has directly exposed volume/CPC data in the accessible datasets; the rest are explicitly marked as estimates and should be pulled from a licensed Google Keyword Planner/Ahrefs/Semrush account before you use them for numerical traffic forecasting.

### The initial site structure

I would launch around these pages:

`/iq-test/` — canonical test
`/free-iq-test/` — free/no-email conversion page
`/10-minute-iq-test/` — quick-test intent
`/iq-test-score/` — score interpretation
`/iq-percentile-calculator/` — calculator utility
`/are-online-iq-tests-accurate/` — trust/evidence hub
`/iq-test-questions/` — questions/practice

That gives the site a coherent topical graph instead of dozens of SEO landing pages.

### One important methodological limitation

I **did not fabricate Google autocomplete/PAA results**. Google US autocomplete/PAA cannot be comprehensively harvested through the search interface available here, and I would rather explicitly mark that as an evidence gap than present invented “PAA keywords.” The report specifies the exact collection workflow using Google Trends, Bing Keyword Research, Google Search Console, and licensed keyword databases. Google documents that Trends data are normalized/sampled rather than raw search volume, while Bing's keyword tool explicitly exposes questions, trends, newly discovered queries and geographic filters. ([Google Help][7])

Likewise, **Ahrefs KD and Semrush KD should not be treated as interchangeable “difficulty scores.”** Ahrefs' KD is primarily backlink-based, while its Keyword Explorer additionally emphasizes Traffic Potential; Semrush exposes its own KD, volume, CPC, intent and SERP information. ([Ahrefs][8])

Google's own guidance strongly supports the architecture recommended here: people-first original content, crawlable internal links, sitemaps, visible HTML content around JavaScript functionality, descriptive titles, and avoidance of scaled search-engine-first pages. ([Google for Developers][9])

The report also treats AI Overview/AI-search CTR as a **planning risk rather than a fixed CTR number** because current studies show substantial query-dependent click changes. ([Ahrefs][10])

**My strategic conclusion:** don't try to beat 123test/IDRlabs/Mensa by becoming a larger quiz directory. Build the **fastest trustworthy free IQ-test experience on the web**, make the result genuinely free, explain exactly what it means, publish the methodology, own the score/percentile/accuracy questions, and use those utility pages to progressively build authority toward the `iq test` head term.
