# 5. ICAR ka scoring actually bahut important hai

# 9. Population norms ke liye 97,000 participants ko blindly use nahi kar sakte

Ye bahut important hai.

Ek later paper explicitly notes:

> ICAR validation sample me young college students significantly overrepresented the, therefore **population norms cannot be adequately generated from that dataset**.

([PubMed Central (PMC)][10])

Yaani:

**97,000 = enormous sample**

but:

**97,000 ≠ representative US population norm sample**

This is one of the most important things I want your agent to understand.

Therefore tum website par ye nahi bol sakte:

> “Our IQ percentile is normed using 97,000 people.”

Unless you have a proper norming methodology and representative sample.

# 10. This also explains our earlier concern about “Mean 100, SD 15”

Mathematical standard IQ scale:

**Mean = 100, SD = 15**

is perfectly legitimate as a standardized scale convention.

But that does **not** mean your current website has empirically established US population norms.

There are two different things:

### Scale transformation

`z → 100 + 15z`

### Norm establishment

“What does a score correspond to in the target population?”

Tum dono ko site content me separate rakhna chahiye.

# 9. One thing I do NOT agree with in the research document

The research says:

> “24-item test targets α ≥ 0.82–0.85” 

and elsewhere:

> “calibrated 24-item test with anticipated reliability ≈ 0.84” 

This is still too optimistic.

You can **target** a reliability level.

You cannot infer that a particular 24-item test **will** achieve α=.84 just from item count.

Reliability depends on actual response covariance, heterogeneity, dimensionality, item characteristics, sample, etc.

### So I would change:

> “targets α ≥ 0.82–0.85”

to:

> **“is designed with the goal of achieving adequate reliability; reliability will be estimated empirically during pilot testing.”**

This is an important correction.

# 11. But there's another subtle scoring issue: MLE + 24 questions

Psychometric spec says:

> Newton-Raphson maximum likelihood
> boundary clamp θ ∈ [-3,+3]. 

This is mathematically implementable.

But for all-correct or all-wrong response patterns, ordinary 2PL MLE has an important problem: **the likelihood may not have a finite interior maximum**.

Hence the clamp is essentially a fallback.

That means a user who gets all 24 correct could hit:

> θ = +3

not because the test truly measured their ability at exactly +3, but because the estimator hit the artificial boundary.

So later, I would actually prefer **EAP/MAP estimation with a stated prior** for the production version, especially at the extremes.

Interestingly, your homepage research originally considered EAP, while the current implementation spec moved to Newton-Raphson. This is something I'd revisit before final psychometric lock.
