3. Apply to Row 3: Column 1 contains a forward diagonal (\). Column 2 contains both a forward diagonal (\) and a backward diagonal (/). The forward diagonal cancels out. What remains is a single backward diagonal line (/).

[H3] Why is the Average IQ Always 100?
[H3] What Actually Changes with Age?

A common misconception is that an older adult has a lower IQ than a 25-year-old. In professional psychometrics, IQ is an age-normed relative ranking, not an absolute raw count.

Standardization tables calibrate examinees exclusively against peers in their identical age bracket. Therefore, the average score for an 18-year-old, a 45-year-old, and a 70-year-old is statistically defined as exactly 100.

While standardized IQ remains constant, the underlying balance of cognitive faculties shifts:

* •Processing Speed & Fluid Logic: Visual reaction speeds and novel matrix induction peak in early adulthood.
 * •Verbal & Crystallized Acumen: Vocabulary, verbal analogies, and domain expertise expand through the 5th and 6th decades.

--- Section 3 ---
[H3] The Flynn Effect: Are Subsequent Generations Scoring Higher?

Named after psychometric researcher James Flynn, the Flynn effect describes the documented rise in raw cognitive test performance observed across industrialized nations throughout the 20th century (approximately 3 IQ points per decade).

Because raw performance shifted upward over time due to enhanced nutrition, ubiquitous formal schooling, and technological complexity, test publishers must periodically re-standardize tests every 10 to 15 years to reset the population mean back to 100.
